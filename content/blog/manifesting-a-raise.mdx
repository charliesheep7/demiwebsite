Most salary negotiation advice focuses on the conversation itself: the number to name, the pause after you say it, the scripts for common objections.

The conversation takes fifteen minutes. What happens in the six months before it is what determines whether you have anything to put on the table.

## What attention practice actually changes

Holding your future professional self in view — the version of you at the next salary tier, doing work that reflects it — changes what you file as relevant. Your brain starts noticing the evidence. The win you'd normally move past without documenting. The project where your contribution was larger than the final email thread shows. The comparison data point a colleague mentioned in passing.

This is how [directed attention works](/blog/does-manifestation-actually-work): it doesn't produce outcomes. It changes what you notice, and what you notice determines what evidence you build.

A [30-second daily ritual](/blog/30-second-daily-ritual) — holding that future professional self clearly in view each morning — is a filter update. Not a belief update. Not an affirmation. A direction your brain starts pointing for the next few hours.

## The evidence problem

Most people go into a salary conversation with almost no specific evidence. They have a vague sense they've done good work. They haven't documented it. They haven't tracked outcomes, connected their contributions to revenue or efficiency numbers, or noted the unsolicited positive feedback.

This isn't a research gap — they did the work. But attention was pointed elsewhere during it.

When you're holding a future self at a higher salary tier in view, you start noticing the specific outputs that belong on that list. Not because manifestation delivers the raise, but because your filter is tuned for it. You catch the compliment and write it down. You think to ask for the outcome data when the project closes. You notice the comparison when someone mentions what a similar role pays elsewhere.

The raise is built in the six months before the ask. The attention practice is what gets you to show up to that building.

## Self-efficacy in negotiation

Research on salary negotiation consistently finds that people who plan to negotiate get better outcomes than those who don't. The gap is especially pronounced in contexts where negotiation is expected but not performed — which turns out to be most professional situations.

The reason most people don't negotiate isn't ignorance of the option. It's low self-efficacy in the specific context. "I'm not the kind of person who asks for more" is an identity claim, not a salary strategy. It holds — until the evidence starts suggesting otherwise.

[Manifesting confidence](/blog/manifesting-confidence) in a professional context follows the same mechanism: you build it through arranged successes, not through belief. Six months of documenting your wins gives you something specific to cite when you're sitting across the table. The confidence to ask is built from the evidence, not the other way around.

## Process rehearsal for the conversation

The conversation itself is worth preparing for. Not by imagining the "yes," but by rehearsing the specific exchange — walking through it until it feels partially familiar.

What you'll say when you open it. What you'll cite. What you'll do with the first "now isn't a great time." How you'll hold the pause after your number.

This is process rehearsal, not outcome visualization. You're not imagining the new paycheck. You're mentally executing the steps of a hard conversation until the room doesn't feel completely new. Novel situations trigger more anxiety and more verbal stumbling than familiar ones. The rehearsal reduces novelty.

[Half-belief is enough to start](/blog/half-belief-is-the-honest-place-to-start). You don't need to feel certain about the outcome. You need to have walked through it enough that the first sentence comes out cleanly.

## The honest sequence

A manifestation practice for a raise is:

- A daily thirty seconds holding the future professional self in view, so you notice the evidence as you build it
- Six months of documenting specific outcomes, contributions, and feedback
- One careful session on market rate for your role and experience
- A process rehearsal of the conversation, not the outcome
- The ask

Nothing magical. But the attention practice is what makes the first four items happen instead of being quietly deferred for another year.
