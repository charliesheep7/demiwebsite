Your phone is already the first thing you look at in the morning. That's either a problem or an opportunity, depending on what's on the screen.

A vision board on your iPhone turns that reflexive first glance into something intentional — not a notification queue, but a brief reminder of what you're actually working toward. Here's how to build one that takes under an hour and holds up past January.

## Three ways to make it

**Option 1: Canva (free, most flexible)**

Canva has a free tier that works well for this. Open the app, create a new design at 1080×1920 (phone wallpaper size), and start dropping in images and text. You can search their stock library or upload your own photos. The result saves directly to your camera roll and can be set as your lock screen.

Takes about 20–30 minutes. Works best if you like to arrange things visually and want control over the layout.

**Option 2: Apple Freeform (already on your phone)**

Freeform is a built-in app on iPhones running iOS 16 and later. It gives you an infinite canvas where you can drag in photos, add text, and rearrange freely — no templates, no constraints. It doesn't export to a wallpaper cleanly, but you can screenshot a portion of it and set that as your lock screen.

Takes about 15 minutes. Works best if you already use Freeform or want something low-commitment.

**Option 3: A dedicated vision board app**

Apps like Perfectly Happy or Hay House's Vision Board let you build a board and then display it as a slideshow or reminder. They add a daily prompt layer on top of the board itself. Worth trying if you want the board to surface more than once a day.

Takes about 30 minutes to set up. Most have a free tier with paid upgrade for features.

## What to put on it

This is where most guides go vague. Here's a concrete way to decide.

Before you open any app, write down three to five things you want in the next year. Not "be happy" — something specific enough that you'd recognize it if it happened. A particular kind of work. A place. A relationship that feels different than it does now. A version of your mornings.

Then for each one, find a single image that makes you feel it rather than just represent it. Not a stock photo of "success." A kitchen you'd actually want to cook in. A neighborhood street you've actually walked down and liked. The cover of the book by an author whose clarity you want.

Five images is enough. More than eight and the board becomes wallpaper, background noise.

Add a short line of text for each one — not an affirmation, but a specific phrase. "Waking up without an alarm" instead of "freedom." "The Tuesday walk" instead of "healthy lifestyle."

## What a vision board does — and doesn't

A vision board works through attention, not attraction. When you look at images of your goals regularly, your brain begins filtering in relevant information it would have previously ignored — [the reticular activating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reticular_formation) is the mechanism. You notice things you would have missed.

What it doesn't do: guarantee outcomes, or replace the actual work of pursuing what you want.

Research on mental imagery also shows that pure positive visualization — imagining the goal without thinking about the obstacles — can actually reduce motivation by creating a false sense of having already arrived. The image works when it's a prompt to act, not a destination you've already visited in your head.

The honest version of [how vision boards work](/blog/do-vision-boards-really-work) is: they direct your attention. Directed attention is useful. That's the whole case.

## Making it a daily habit

A vision board you save to your phone and forget is not useful. You need a way to see it.

The simplest: set it as your lock screen. Every time you pick up your phone, you see it. This works because you're not adding a new habit — you're redirecting an existing one.

If you want something more deliberate, schedule a one-minute check-in each morning. Open the board, look at each image, and pick one small thing you can move toward today. This isn't a [45-minute morning ritual](/blog/30-second-daily-ritual) — one minute, a decision, done.

For people who find vision boards too static, [a vision board without images](/blog/manifestation-without-vision-boards) covers what to try instead — written prompts, audio, or a different structure entirely.

## A note on the apps that do this daily for you

If making a board from scratch sounds like too much, some apps just show you an image or a prompt each morning and ask you to sit with it for thirty seconds.

That's essentially what [Demi](https://demimanifest.com) does — except instead of an image you found online, it's a brief moment of holding your own future self in view. Thirty seconds. No arrangement required. The goal is the same: to start the day pointed in the right direction rather than pointed at nothing.

Try it alongside a vision board or instead of one. Either works on an ordinary Tuesday.
