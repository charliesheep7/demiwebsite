Physical vision boards get made in January and buried by April. A shoe box of magazine clippings, a corkboard that faces the closet door — they work for about two weeks, until the closet door stays closed.

The appeal of doing it in Google Slides is practical: your laptop is already open. Your phone already has the app. You don't need scissors, cork, or a flat surface in a small apartment.

## Why digital boards actually get looked at

A vision board is an attention tool. It works — when it works — by priming the brain's relevance filter. When you look at images of what you want, consistently, your brain starts updating what it scans for in daily life. You notice the relevant job listing. You say yes to the gym invitation when you'd normally decline.

The looking is the whole practice.

If the board is behind a door, you don't look at it. If it's a pinned tab or a file you open each morning alongside your inbox, you might actually glance at it on a Tuesday while the coffee brews.

[Psychology Today notes](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/click-here-happiness/202103/what-is-vision-board-and-why-make-one) that vision boards gain their power through daily review, not through the initial creation process. A board you spend two hours crafting and never open again does nothing. A board you open for thirty seconds on the commute does something.

## How to set up yours in Google Slides

Open Google Slides and create a blank presentation. Under File → Page setup → Custom, change the dimensions to 1920×1080. That gives you a widescreen layout that fills most screens without awkward white bars.

Then build it one section at a time:

**One slide per life area.** Career, health, relationships, creative work. Don't cram everything onto one slide — it becomes visual noise. Four or five focused slides beats a single cluttered collage.

**Images with intention.** Right-click any slide and go to Insert → Image → Search the web. Choose images that feel specific to your actual life, not images that look like someone else's. A workspace that sounds like rest. A neighborhood you want to walk through.

**A few words, not a wall.** Three to five words per slide, maximum. "More mornings like that." "Work that matters." Affirmations lose their pull when there are too many of them — the [affirmation board research](/blog/affirmation-board) is consistent on this.

**Open it daily.** Pin it as a browser tab, or name the file something you'll click on. Set a thirty-second morning alarm labeled "vision board." The whole method only works if you actually look at it.

Free templates exist on [Slidesgo](https://slidesgo.com/vision-board) if you want a starting layout, though a blank slide honestly takes less time to set up than downloading and editing someone else's design.

## The honest limit

Looking at a vacation photo does not book the flight. That's the part manifestation culture consistently skips.

Vision boards don't work through attraction — some idea that the universe delivers what you focus on. The mechanism is attentional: consistent looking primes the brain to notice more opportunities related to what you've been viewing. That's real, and it's useful, but it requires you to act on what you notice.

As [the research on vision boarding and motivation](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/click-here-happiness/202103/what-is-vision-board-and-why-make-one) puts it, imagery paired with action planning produces measurable shifts in goal-directed behavior. Passive staring at pretty images shows no such effect.

A Slides board is better than a Pinterest board for one practical reason: it's contained. Pinterest pulls you into a scroll loop. A Slides file you open, look at for thirty seconds, and close. If the [daily review is the ritual](/blog/30-second-daily-ritual), the tool should support that kind of brevity rather than swallow thirty minutes in a recommendation feed.

## Keeping it current

Update it every season, or when a goal completes or changes. Stale images that no longer represent what you actually want become irrelevant — your brain stops priming for them.

A goal that completes deserves to be removed. The practice isn't about holding the same images forever. It's about staying honest with yourself about what you're aiming at, updated as often as that aim shifts.

## One slide, tomorrow morning

You don't need a complete life vision to start. One area. Two or three images. One slide. Open it tomorrow morning and look at it for the time it takes to read a text message.

That's the practice. The Google Slides wrapper is just where it lives.

If you've tried vision boards before and they didn't stick, the problem usually isn't the tool — it's [whether you believed you needed to do it perfectly](/blog/half-belief-is-the-honest-place-to-start). Half a vision board, opened regularly, works better than a beautiful one that sits closed.
